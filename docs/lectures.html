Lectures

Slides for the 2026/27 run, downloadable as HTML or PDF, and the archive of previous years

Each lecture is an HTML presentation (open it in the browser, press F for full screen, E for a print-ready view, ? for help). It can also be downloaded as a single self-contained HTML file that works offline, or as a PDF with one page per slide. Lectures 4 and 12 are new this year and are being prepared. The slides of previous years, 2025 included, are in the archive.

The slides of lectures 5–11 still carry last year’s numbers 4–10 on their title page; they will be renumbered once the new lecture 4 is in place. The content is current.

Lectures 2026/27

Lecture 1

Introduction, optimization, how we code now

What machine learning is; supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning. Distances, 1-NN, k-NN, k-means, UPGMA. Optimization: convexity, closed-form vs. iterative solutions. Why we compute by hand and validate what an agent writes.

Tomáš Vičar

Slides Download HTML Download PDF

Lecture 2

Performance evaluation, metrics, preprocessing

The supervised pipeline; train / validation / test. Confusion-matrix metrics, ROC and precision–recall, R². Class imbalance, cross-validation, scaling, missing values, outliers, and where information leaks.

Tomáš Vičar

not published yet

Lecture 3

Feature extraction, selection and dimensionality reduction

Curse of dimensionality. Filter methods (correlation, entropy, mutual information, mRMR), wrappers (forward selection, backward elimination), embedded methods. PCA, LDA, t-SNE and UMAP, feature expansion.

Tomáš Vičar

not published yet

Lecture 4 · new

Vibecoding — how coding agents work

What a coding agent is: context, tools, the agent loop. What the models can and cannot do. Specifying a task, reviewing a diff, tests as the contract, version control as the safety net. Telling correct code from plausible-looking code.

in preparation
not published yet

Lecture 5

Linear models and kernels

Linear regression and linear classification as one equation. OLS, LAD, ridge and lasso; polynomial regression. The kernel trick, primal vs. dual ridge regression. LDA, MSE classifier, logistic regression, perceptron, SVM with hard and soft margin, kernel SVM.

Tomáš Vičar

not published yet

Lecture 6

Decision trees and ensembles

Bias–variance trade-off and learning curves. CART for classification and regression: impurity, greedy splitting, stopping. Bagging, random forest and out-of-bag samples. AdaBoost step by step.

Jiří Chmelík

not published yet

Lecture 7

Artificial neural networks

Perceptron and the artificial neuron; activation functions. Gradient for one neuron, matrix notation, the general network. Mini-batch gradient descent, Adam, saddle points. Loss functions. L1/L2 regularization, dropout, batch normalization.

Jiří Chmelík

not published yet

Lecture 8

Convolutional networks 1 — basics

Shallow vs. deep networks and the price of depth. From fully connected to convolutional: local connections, shared weights, counting parameters. Convolutional layer, activation, pooling, dropout, batch norm; a typical CNN layer by layer.

Jiří Chmelík

not published yet

Lecture 9

Convolutional networks 2 — special blocks

Skip connections and residual blocks. 1 × 1 convolution, bottlenecks, autoencoders. Dilated convolution and the receptive field. Up-sampling and transposed convolution. Channel and spatial attention, CBAM.

Roman Jakubíček

not published yet

Lecture 10

Other networks — architectures and applications

Regression and classification heads, encoders. Image-to-image networks, segmentation (U-Net, nnU-Net), object detection (Faster R-CNN). Recurrent networks and LSTM. Transfer learning, pre-trained networks, augmentation.

Jiří Chmelík · Roman Jakubíček

not published yet

Lecture 11

Transformers

Tokenization, embeddings, positional encoding. Self-attention in three steps: queries, keys, values; multi-head attention and its cost. Encoder, decoder, output head. Vision transformer. Pre-trained transformer models.

Jiří Chmelík

not published yet

Lecture 12 · new

Generative models and wrap-up

Proposal: diffusion models (forward and reverse process, denoising as the training objective), a short look at how large language models are trained (pre-training, fine-tuning, RLHF), and a summary of the course.

in preparation
