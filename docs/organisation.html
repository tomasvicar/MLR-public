Assessment

The course is worth 5 credits: 100 points in total, 30 from the semester and 70 from the final oral exam. The binding version is the course statement.

Semester — 30 points, at least 15 for the credit

Six graded items, 5 points each. Dates are announced in the e-learning at the start of the semester.

Item Points What it covers
Theoretical test 1 5 multiple choice; lectures and labs up to that point
Theoretical test 2 5 multiple choice; lectures and labs up to that point
Theoretical test 3 5 multiple choice; lectures and labs up to that point
Practical test — computing 5 by hand: metrics from a confusion matrix, a distance and a k-NN decision, one forward-selection step, a closed-form fit
Practical test — red flags 5 find what is wrong in a given pipeline or agent-written code: leakage, scaling before the split, test set used for model selection, wrong metric
Project 5 ranking of your model on a hidden test set among the other students; one agreed metric

Theoretical tests are taken in the e-learning: 5 questions, 1 point each, one question per page without going back, 8 minutes, no materials. An answer that mixes correct and incorrect options gets zero points; a partially correct selection gets a proportional part of the point. You may ask for a translation or explanation of a question during the test. Retakes are not possible.

Credit requirements

  • attendance in all computer labs, with at most two excused absences (a missed lab cannot normally be made up at another time),
  • at least 15 points from the semester,
  • coming to the labs with knowledge of the topics already covered in the lectures.

Final exam — 70 points, at least 35 to pass

Oral exam of 20–30 minutes with two examiners. The topics are split into two areas; you draw one question from each area and each examiner grades one of them. The final score is the average of the two, but a substantial lack of knowledge in either area means zero points for the whole exam. You do not have to memorise equations, but you must be able to explain and interpret every equation on the slides. The exam can be taken in Czech or English. Erasmus students who cannot register for a full date should e-mail the guarantor.

The list of topics from the last exam is on the materials page; the 2026/27 list is published before the exam period.

Rules for using AI

  • Labs 1–4: switch the AI assistance in Colab off (Settings → AI assistance). You write the code yourself, because the point of these labs is to understand what the algorithm does — including when the code is later written by an agent.
  • Labs 5–12: a coding agent is part of the work. The faculty provides access, so no subscription of your own is needed; the setup is done in lab 5. Git is mandatory from lab 5 on.
  • Tests: no AI tools and no materials during the theoretical tests. The practical tests are about understanding and checking code, which is exactly what you have to do with an agent’s output anyway.
  • Project: you may use any tool; the result is what is graded. Be able to explain every line you submit.

Workload

One credit corresponds to 26 hours of work, so five credits are roughly two hours of lectures, two hours of lab and about four hours of your own preparation per week, plus the preparation for the exam (directive on credits).
