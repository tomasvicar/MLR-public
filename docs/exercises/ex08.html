Exercise 8 — PyTorch

Last week’s network rebuilt in PyTorch step by step, then a leaf classifier from the files on disk to your phone: fine-tune a pretrained network and let an agent put it on a web page, write a pipeline yourself, then make the classifier as good as you can

Builds on the lectures on artificial neural networks and convolutional networks, and starts where Task 5 of exercise 7 ended.

! Compulsory hand-in: complete and submit Task 2 and the report of Task 4 within 48 hours after your lab. Attendance will not be credited unless you complete this requirement.

When to use AI agents

  • Task 1: no agent needed. Everything is written: run the cells together with the teacher and read them.
  • Task 2: run the demonstration, then use your coding agent. Run the cells together with the teacher; the agent writes and publishes the phone page, you check it and write REVIEW.md yourself.
  • Task 3: work without AI agents. Write your own Dataset, DataLoader, network and training loop; look at Tasks 1 and 2 whenever you are stuck.
  • Task 4: use your coding agent. It writes the improvements, you decide what to try, judge the results and write the report yourself. Tell it to keep your simple, readable code and to add or change only what each variant needs.

What you will build

  • Last week’s sine network rebuilt one PyTorch piece at a time — batches, nn.Module, a loss function and an optimizer, Dataset and DataLoader, the GPU, evaluation and saving — fully worked, run together.
  • A leaf classifier (apple, cherry, chestnut, maple): a pretrained MobileNet fine-tuned in PyTorch on the leaves your group photographed, its mistakes in a confusion matrix, exported to ONNX — and, with an agent, a public GitHub Pages page that runs it on your phone’s live camera.
  • Your own pipeline: your own Dataset, a DataLoader, a small network as an nn.Module class and the training loop with validation; then augmentation and batch normalization, measured on validation.
  • With an agent: as many improvements as you can, each measured against a baseline, also on camera-like images, in a short report REPORT.md.

Before the lab

  • Have a Google account ready: at the start of the lab you photograph leaves with your phone and upload them — see Your leaves below.
  • Have last week’s notebook at hand: Task 1 starts where its Task 5 (tensors, autograd, no_grad(), the device) ended.
  • Re-read the lecture on convolutional networks: convolution, padding, max pooling and the fully connected layer at the end.
  • Bring the laptop and the coding agent from exercise 5, and your phone. Check that the agent starts and that gh auth status confirms your GitHub login. In Colab, PyTorch is preinstalled; a GPU is optional (Runtime → Change runtime type → T4 GPU, before you run the first cell); on the CPU the MobileNet training takes about a minute.

2. The model on your phone, with an agent

The notebook exports the trained network into leaf_model.onnx and packs it with model_info.json (classes and preprocessing), a check image and the probabilities the notebook computes for it into ex08_for_agent.zip. Your agent writes one index.html that runs the model in the phone’s browser (ONNX Runtime Web), creates the public repository mlr-ex08, and switches on GitHub Pages. The notebook gives a starting prompt, the requirements and how you check each one in REVIEW.md.

With the CERIT models use the agentic model (coder cannot look at images). If the agent stops with a server error, run it again or type continue.

Training on a Colab GPU from your agent

Optional, for Task 4: your agent can keep working locally and send only the training to a free Colab T4 GPU through Google’s official colab CLI. The guide explains it and installs the two agent skills with one prompt; you log in to Google once yourself.

Data

Your leaves

The lab runs on your group’s own photographs, taken at its start. You get the leaves in the lab:

  1. For each of the four species — apple, cherry, chestnut and maple — take 2 different leaves and one photo of each. That is 8 photos from you.
  2. One leaf per photo, the leaf filling most of the frame. Any phone and any format are fine (iPhone HEIC too).
  3. Upload them into your group’s folder, into the folder of the species (apple, cherry, chestnut or maple — the folder decides the class), inside a subfolder with your name: group 1, group 2, group 3, group 4.

The teacher packs each group’s photos into one ZIP on their Google Drive and gives you its shared file link. Paste it into GROUP_ZIP in the notebook; the link is required before downloading the data. The split into training and validation keeps a student’s photos of one species together, so the validation tests the network on leaves it has never seen, photographed by somebody else.

Last years’ photographs

ex08_leaves_images.zip (2.3 MB) — 820 photographs of the same four species taken by the students of this course in previous years, 175 + 30 per class in a training and a validation folder, 96 × 96 pixels. In Task 4 they are optional extra training data and a test on other people’s leaves. They do not replace your group’s ZIP when its link is missing.

Hand-in

! Compulsory hand-in: complete and submit Task 2 and the report of Task 4 within 48 hours after your lab. Attendance will not be credited unless you complete this requirement.

A public GitHub repository mlr-ex08 with GitHub Pages switched on, and MLRteacher invited as a collaborator (Settings → Collaborators; a pending invitation is enough). It contains:

  1. index.html, leaf_model.onnx, model_info.json, check_image.jpg and check_expected.json;
  2. README.md with the URL of your page;
  3. REVIEW.md, written by you: a screenshot from your phone, the probabilities of check_image.jpg on the page next to the notebook’s, and what you found in the code for each requirement in the notebook;
  4. REPORT.md of Task 4, written by you: the table of what you tried, what each change brought, whether it is larger than the noise, what did not help, and which model you would put on the phone.

What is pushed before the deadline counts. The repository is public — put nothing personal in it.
